Not Medical Advice

Keeper is an educational and personal-tracking tool. It is not a medical device, and nothing in the app — including period, ovulation, fertility, pregnancy, or postpartum predictions, symptom and mood logging, nutrition and exercise content, or any other feature — constitutes medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Using Keeper does not create a doctor-patient relationship between you and Keeper, its developers, or any source cited within the app.

Predictions Are Estimates, Not Facts

Keeper's cycle, fertility, ovulation, and pregnancy-related predictions are calculated from your own logged data using general statistical and clinical conventions (for example, a 14-day luteal-phase assumption, Naegele's rule for due dates). They are estimates that can be wrong, sometimes significantly — due to irregular cycles, incomplete logging, underlying health conditions, or inherent limits of the calculation methods themselves. Do not rely on Keeper's predictions for:

  • Determining whether you are pregnant
  • Preventing or achieving pregnancy
  • Timing medical procedures or medications
  • Any decision where being wrong could affect your health or safety

Educational Content

Keeper displays educational content drawn from sources such as the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), Mayo Clinic, and Cleveland Clinic. Where content is explicitly marked as draft or pending clinical review within the app, it has not yet been confirmed by a licensed clinician and should be treated with additional caution. Even reviewed content:

  • May not reflect the most current medical guidance
  • May not apply to your individual health history, medications, or circumstances
  • Is not a substitute for personalized advice from your own healthcare provider

Always Consult Your Healthcare Provider

Always seek the advice of a qualified physician or other healthcare provider with any questions you have regarding a medical condition, pregnancy, postpartum recovery, contraception, or any symptom you log in the app. Never disregard professional medical advice or delay seeking it because of something you read or saw in Keeper.

Emergencies

Keeper is not an emergency service and cannot alert anyone on your behalf. If you are experiencing a medical emergency — including but not limited to the warning signs listed in the app (such as severe bleeding, chest pain, trouble breathing, thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, or others) — call 911 (or your local emergency number) or go to the nearest emergency room immediately. If you are in crisis or having thoughts of self-harm, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) in the U.S.

Symptom and Mood Logging

Tags you log (symptoms, moods, and related entries) are self-reported and for your own personal tracking and pattern recognition only. Features that reference "common" symptoms or compare your logs to published research are drawing on general population data, never on other Keeper users' information, and are not a screening or diagnostic tool.

Voice Notes and Transcription

If you use Keeper's voice note feature, on-device transcription converts your recording to text, and a keyword scan highlights sentences that may sound like instructions. This highlighting is a plain word-matching tool, not an AI-generated summary or clinical interpretation — it may miss important information or highlight irrelevant text. Always rely on the full transcript or your own recording, not the highlights alone, for anything medically important.
